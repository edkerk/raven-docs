# raven-docs

## Documentation prose style

This site's readers are not all native English speakers. Prose has to be
direct and literal, not just factually correct.

No idioms, figures of speech, or headline-style fragments that drop the
subject or verb for effect. A heading or sentence should parse the same way
for a reader translating it word by word as it does for a native speaker.
Two examples flagged and rewritten in a live sweep of this repo:

- "Names do not convert mechanically" (a heading) implies its meaning through
  word choice ("mechanically" standing in for "automatically" or "by a fixed
  rule") rather than stating it.
- "Same verdicts, very different cost" (a heading) is a sentence fragment: the
  subject and verb are implied, not written, and the reader has to supply
  "they give" and "but they have" to parse it.

Rewrite this kind of phrasing as a complete, literal sentence that says what
it means. Direct is not the same as dumbed down: keep the technical content
and the precision, drop only the cleverness.

No em dashes, anywhere: not in prose, headings, table cells, or generated
strings. Use the punctuation the sentence calls for instead: a comma where
the clause continues, a semicolon where it is independent, a colon where
what follows defines or enumerates what precedes it, parentheses for a
matched pair around an aside.
